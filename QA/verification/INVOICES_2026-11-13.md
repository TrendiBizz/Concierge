# Invoices from an accepted quote

An accepted quote was a dead end: the customer could accept, the owner could mark it paid, but
nothing was ever billed. Every competitor produces an invoice; a tradesman cannot ask for money
without one.

## What exists now

* **`invoices` table** — number, kind (`full` | `deposit`), status, subtotal/GST/total, **due now**,
  deposit, balance, currency, lines snapshot, issue date, due date, public token.
* **`api/app/invoices.py`**
  * `split(total, kind, percent)` — what is due now and what waits for completion. Integer half-up
    rounding, so the two invoices always add back to the total exactly.
  * `next_number()` — atomic per-business sequence (`PUK-0001`, `T-0004`), never reused.
  * `issue()` — creates the invoice for an accepted quote; terms come from the business (default 7
    days) to set the due date.
  * `page()` — the customer's invoice: issuer block (name, address, phone, GST number, NZBN), number,
    issue and due dates, line items, subtotal, **GST**, total, **Due now**, and for a deposit invoice
    "balance remains due on completion".
* **Customer chooses how to pay, when they accept** — "Accept quotation — pay in full" and
  "Accept and pay 50% deposit ($2,910.94)" (percentage is the business's). The choice travels in the
  URL (`/quote/{token}/accept?option=deposit`), because a posted body can be consumed by middleware
  before the handler reads it.
* **Routes** — `GET /invoice/{token}` (public, noindex), `GET /owner/invoices` (owner list).
* **Gateway** — `/invoice/*` added to the `@owner` path allow-list, gateway recreated.
* **Portal** — business details card gained deposit %, invoice payment terms, and a deposits on/off
  toggle.

## No Stripe

There is no payment processor in the product: the only "stripe" reference is a marketing link on the
website (`products.js`). Invoices are issued by us and paid however the business already takes money;
the existing "I have paid" → owner confirmation flow records settlement. That decision is unchanged
and deliberate — nothing here depends on a paid service, and the schema stores amounts and dates,
not card data, so a processor can be added later without a rewrite.

## Live proof

Throwaway quote on `trendi-support` (since deleted), $5,821.88 total, GST $759.38:

* customer page offered both choices, deposit shown as **2,910.94 NZD**;
* accepting with `?option=deposit` returned 200 and produced invoice **T-0004**, `kind=deposit`,
  `due_cents=291094`, `balance_cents=291094`;
* `GET /invoice/zE7Zl9…` returned **200** containing "Deposit invoice", "2,910.94", "remains due",
  "5,821.88", "GST", and the cladding line.

## Faults found and fixed on the way

1. **Invoice page 500 — a column that does not exist.** It selected `tenants.currency_hint`. A new
   guard (`tests/test_tenant_columns_exist.py`) parses every `SELECT … FROM tenants` string literal and
   checks the columns against `schema.sql`; proved by putting the bad column back — the guard names
   `invoices.py:currency_hint` and fails.
2. **Lines double-encoded.** asyncpg returns `jsonb` as text, and `json.dumps(text)` stored a JSON
   *string*, so the page crashed on `line.get`. Parsed on write and on read now.
3. **The customer's deposit choice was silently downgraded to full price.** The choice lived only in
   the posted form; when the body was unavailable the handler fell back to `full`, and a customer who
   chose a 50% deposit was invoiced the whole job. Fixed by carrying the choice in the URL with the
   body as fallback — verified `payment_option=deposit` and `kind=deposit` in the database.

Tests: 9 in `test_invoices.py` (split maths, rounding, clamping, numbering, description, the choice,
tax-invoice content, routes) plus 3 column guards. **1101 passing.**
