llms.txt vs robots.txt: what each file does

robots.txt is a long-standing standard that tells crawlers which parts of a site they may access, and compliant crawlers read it before they fetch pages. llms.txt is different: it's an optional, proposed file that summarises a site in plain text for tools that may choose to read it. Neither file is a ranking switch. Google has said it does not use llms.txt for Search (Google Search Central). So keep robots.txt accurate first, and treat llms.txt as a small extra, not a fix for crawl or content problems.

What does Google say about llms.txt?

Google’s Search Central guide on generative AI features (covered by Search Engine Land, 15 May 2026) states that AI Overviews and AI Mode are rooted in core Search ranking systems. In the mythbusting section, Google lists things you can ignore for Google Search:

  • llms.txt and other “special” AI markup
  • Content “chunking” for AI
  • Rewriting content just for AI systems
  • Seeking inauthentic brand mentions
  • Overfocusing on special schema for AI

Google later clarified that maintaining llms.txt for other services is fine. It will not harm or help Google rankings because Google Search ignores the file.

Do studies show llms.txt helps AI citations?

Vendor and industry analyses of large domain sets have reported little or no correlation between hosting llms.txt and AI citations, and AI crawlers rarely request the file. Treat those percentages as directional (many come from GEO vendors). The practical takeaway matches Google: foundational SEO and third-party corroboration matter far more.

FAQ

Does llms.txt guarantee AI mentions?

No. It is an optional file some tools may read, with no ranking or AI-mention promise.

What should I fix first?

Start with crawl access, a valid sitemap and accurate, useful pages. Use the Trendi site snapshot to check public signals.

What is the difference between llms.txt and robots.txt?

robots.txt tells compliant crawlers which parts of your site they may access. llms.txt is an optional, proposed file that summarises your site for tools that may read it. One controls crawler access; the other is a summary. Neither guarantees rankings, citations or AI mentions.

Is llms.txt worth it?

It's optional, but Google has said it does not use it for Search; see Google Search Central for the statement. Fix crawl access, accurate content and your sitemap first. Add llms.txt afterwards if you want, without expecting a particular result.
