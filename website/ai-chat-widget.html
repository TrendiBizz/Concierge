Questions

How do I add the chat widget?

Add the script before the closing body tag on your site. We provide the exact snippet and can test it with you.

Does it answer from my website?

Yes. We load the public pages you own into its knowledge and it answers from that information. If the answer is not there, it offers to pass the question on.

Can it book appointments?

Yes, for agreed hours and rules, once booking is enabled by the owner.

How much does it cost?

USD $250 a month with a 3-month minimum. Phone and SMS provider charges are separate.

Is the chat private?

Each business has a private CRM workspace. The chat API is origin-locked to your site and conversations are stored for your business.

Will the widget slow my website down?

It is a single deferred script served from Trendi’s domain, and we test the page with it installed before activation. We cannot promise a zero-cost download, so we measure rather than claim.

Can it answer in languages other than English?

The assistant answers in English today. Other languages need to be configured and tested with real questions before we would claim them, so ask before you assume.

What happens when it does not know the answer?

It says so, offers to take the visitor’s details for a person to reply, and records the question. Those unanswered questions are the most useful part of the report: they tell you what your site should say next.

Does it collect personal information without consent?

Contact details are only asked for when the visitor wants a booking, a callback or a quote, and the workspace records what was captured and why. Retention and integrations are agreed before activation.

What makes a website chat widget useful instead of annoying

Most chat widgets fail for the same three reasons: they answer from a generic script rather than your business, they ask for contact details before they have given anything, and there is no way for a person to take over. A useful widget is narrow and honest. It answers what your own pages already answer, asks for one contact detail only when the visitor wants a booking or a callback, and hands over the moment the question leaves the script.

Concierge is pointed at your public pages and configured with the questions your customers actually ask, so the answer to "do you deliver on Saturdays" comes from your own delivery page rather than an invented policy.

How the knowledge base is built and kept current

The widget is only as accurate as the information loaded into it, so we treat content as part of setup rather than a one-off import:

  1. We agree which public pages it may use — services, pricing, hours, delivery, FAQs.
  2. We load and test them with a set of real customer questions, including the awkward ones.
  3. We record where it could not answer, because that list is a content to-do for your site.
  4. When you change a page or a price, the knowledge is refreshed — that is the step most chat deployments skip, and it is why they go stale.

Install, performance and privacy

Install is one script tag before the closing body tag, or a message to your web developer. There is no theme change and no plugin. The script is served from Trendi's own domain, and the chat API is origin-locked to your site so another domain cannot use your key.

Each business has a private workspace with owner, editor and viewer roles. Conversations and contact details stay with your business. What we cannot promise is that it will never slow your page: we keep the script small and load it deferred, and we will test your site with it installed before you go live. Retention and any integration are agreed before activation — see privacy and security.

Measuring whether it is working

Four numbers are enough to judge a chat widget: how many conversations it had, how many questions it answered without a handover, how many bookings or callbacks came out of it, and how long it took to first reply. All four are visible in the workspace. If the handover rate is high, the site is missing content; if conversations are high and bookings are zero, the ask is wrong.
