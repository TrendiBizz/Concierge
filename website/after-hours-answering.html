What happens after hours

When a customer calls or messages outside your opening hours, Concierge answers from your business information, takes their name and contact details, offers an agreed appointment time where appropriate, and records the conversation in your CRM.

You choose the hours it covers and how your team is notified. If a question needs a person, it says so and captures the details for a callback.

Why after-hours enquiries matter

Many customers search and enquire when their own day ends. A missed call at 7pm can become a booking for a competitor by 9am. Answering and recording the enquiry keeps the opportunity with your business.

Questions

What is an after-hours answering service?

It answers customer calls and messages outside normal business hours, captures the enquiry and passes it to the team. Concierge is an AI assistant that does this from your own business information.

How much does it cost?

Concierge is USD $250 a month with a 3-month minimum. Phone and SMS provider charges are separate. Website chat is included.

Can it book appointments after hours?

Yes, for agreed appointment times and hours. You choose the availability and appointment length; the booking is kept with the enquiry.

Will it answer every call?

It answers the calls and messages you enable. A phone number and voice provider must be connected and tested. Complex conversations are passed to a person with the context.

Does it promise more bookings?

No. It helps make sure after-hours enquiries are answered and recorded. Demand and conversion are outside our control.

Does after-hours answering cover weekends and public holidays?

Yes, if you enable it. You set the opening hours, the weekend window and the public-holiday behaviour, and Concierge answers only inside those windows. Anything outside them goes to voicemail or to a number you nominate.

Can it tell an emergency from a routine enquiry?

Only by the rules you give it. You can define the phrases and topics that must be escalated immediately, and it follows them. It is not a medical or safety triage system, and it should never be relied on as one.

What happens to a call it cannot answer?

It says it does not know, offers to take the details so a person can reply, and records the question in the CRM. It is configured not to invent an answer, a price or an appointment slot.

Is after-hours answering available without a phone number?

The phone channel needs a number and a voice provider, which varies by country. Website chat and email capture work without one, and every channel is tested before activation.

Why an unanswered after-hours call costs more than it looks

A call that rings out at 8pm does not appear anywhere you normally look. There is no form submission to count, no chat transcript to read, and usually no voicemail worth transcribing. The only record is a missed-call notification on a phone that is in somebody's pocket at home. The enquiry either rings the next number on the list or never comes back.

That is the gap after-hours answering closes: not a better website, not more traffic, just making sure the message a customer left at the moment they were ready to buy gets recorded and answered while it still matters. What happens when someone calls after hours, step by step

  1. The call is answered. A connected business number is picked up by Concierge, which introduces itself as an AI assistant.
  2. The question is understood. It answers from the business information you approved: hours, location, services, pricing rules, how the booking process works.
  3. Contact details are captured. Name plus a phone number or email, with the reason for the call, and permission to follow up.
  4. An appointment is offered. Only times you have enabled, with the length and buffer rules you set. The booking is kept with the enquiry.
  5. Anything sensitive is handed over. Urgent, clinical, legal, complaint or high-value conversations are passed to a person with the context, not guessed at.
  6. The record is written. The conversation, the contact details and the next action land in the CRM, so the morning starts with a list instead of a voicemail box.

What you decide before it goes live

An after-hours assistant is only as good as the rules behind it, and those rules are yours, not ours:

  • Opening hours and holidays. When it answers, when it stays silent, and what happens on a public holiday.
  • The greeting and the disclosure. How it identifies itself, and whether callers are told it is an AI. We recommend being plain about it — pretending otherwise damages trust the first time it matters.
  • Escalation. Which numbers it can reach, in what order, and what counts as urgent enough to interrupt someone.
  • Appointment rules. Which hours are bookable, appointment lengths, gaps between appointments, and what it should do when nothing fits.
  • Recording and retention. Whether calls are recorded, how the caller is asked, where transcripts are stored and how long they are kept. Retention is agreed with you before activation, not after.

Where an after-hours assistant is the wrong tool

It is the wrong choice for emergencies that need a human immediately, for complaints that need judgement, and for negotiation. It is also a poor fit if your enquiries are so few that a person can reasonably answer the phone at night. We would rather say that than sell you something that annoys your customers.
