Why the New Zealand case is its own case

Most of the AI receptionist and AI office market is written for companies with a front desk and a receptionist sitting at it. A builder in Hamilton, a plumber in Tauranga or a two-person clinic in Dunedin does not have that. The person who would answer the phone is on a roof, under a house, or with a patient.

Three things follow, and they are all New Zealand-shaped rather than generic:

  • The best enquiries arrive when you are closed. Working couples call after dinner. Saturday morning is when people finally get to the jobs list. A business that is reachable 7am to 6pm and silent at 7pm loses the call to whoever answers at 7pm.
  • The caller already knows the alternative is a competitor. New Zealand trades and clinics are densely served. Nobody drives 40 minutes for a plumber they cannot reach, and they do not leave a voicemail twice.
  • The time difference cuts both ways. For a business here, New Zealand daylight saving shifts the working day against Australian and US callers twice a year. Set times that were right in June are an hour out in December. That is a configuration problem, not a customer complaint to absorb.

Our own business is registered in New Zealand (TRENDI LIMITED), which is why this page exists: it is the market we can support with the least pretending.

What it does on a real New Zealand working day

  • Answers the calls you cannot. On site, driving, with a customer, after hours. It answers from information you approve — services, suburbs you cover, hours, how quoting works — rather than guessing.
  • Takes the job, not just the message. Name, number, address or suburb, what the job is, how urgent, and whether they own or rent where that matters. Enough for you to ring back and quote, instead of ringing back to ask.
  • Handles the same six questions. Do you do leaky homes? What is your call-out? Can you come Thursday? Are you taking new clients? The repetitive part of the day is the part that gets answered consistently at 9pm.
  • Texts back the ones it could not finish. A missed-call text back keeps the thread alive when the call drops or the caller hangs up mid-menu.
  • Books agreed times. Within the rules you set, for clinics, real estate viewings and trades where the first appointment is an assessment.
  • Passes anything sensitive to a person. Complaints, pricing arguments, insurance disputes, clinical questions. The escalation rules are written with you and tested before switching on.

It runs on website chat, phone and email. Phone and SMS need a provider and a number that can be routed to the platform; website chat works without one. We test each channel with you before it answers a real customer.

Privacy, in New Zealand terms

Concierge collects personal information — names, contact details, and what the caller said about their job or appointment. Under the Privacy Act 2020 that information comes with obligations that stay with your business, not with a software vendor. We are not your privacy officer and do not claim to be.

What we can tell you plainly, and what you should ask any supplier of this kind:

  • Each business gets its own workspace; staff access is role-controlled.
  • What is collected, why, and how long it is kept is set out in the privacy policy; retention and any integration with your own systems are agreed in writing before activation.
  • Call recording asks the caller first, and calls that are not recorded say so.
  • Transcripts stay with the enquiry and can be exported.
  • Where the data is held, and which sub-processors are involved, is in the policy rather than in a sales call. Security covers access and vulnerability reporting.

Who this fits, and who it does not

It fits businesses where the phone is the front door and the person who answers it is also the person doing the work: builders and trades, clinics and practices, real estate, service businesses with a van and a booking calendar.

It does not fit a business whose calls are mostly complaints, whose pricing has to be negotiated live, or where every conversation needs a licensed professional from the first sentence. In those cases an answering service staffed by people is the honest recommendation, and we say so on AI answering service vs human.

Frequently asked

Does it understand a New Zealand accent?

Yes, and it is configured with your own place names and trade terms, which is where recognition actually fails — street names, suburb spellings, brand names — rather than on accent alone. We test with real questions from your business before it answers a customer.

Can it handle the daylight saving change?

Yes. Hours are configured with the correct New Zealand time zone, so the change is a setting rather than a surprise. If you run different hours in summer, say so at setup.

Will it answer calls from overseas?

It answers the number you route to it regardless of where the caller is. We do not claim the platform is limited to New Zealand callers; we do claim this is the market we know well enough to configure properly.

What if it gets something wrong?

Every conversation is recorded in the enquiry with a transcript, so a mistake is visible and fixable rather than invisible. Escalation rules are reviewed after the first weeks of live calls and adjusted.
