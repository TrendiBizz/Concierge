SECURITY & CONFIDENCE

Vulnerability disclosure

Direct answer: If you find a security issue on trendibizz.com or related Trendi systems we operate, email admin@trendibizz.com.

We prefer coordinated disclosure: tell us first, give us a reasonable window to investigate and fix, then publish if you choose.

How to report

  • Email admin@trendibizz.com with a clear description, affected URL or component, and steps to reproduce where safe.
  • Include your preferred contact method if it is not the From address.
  • Do not include passwords, personal data of third parties, or exploit chains that would harm customers.

What we ask

  • Act in good faith. Do not access data that is not yours, and do not degrade the service.
  • Avoid social engineering of staff or customers, physical attacks, and denial-of-service testing unless we agreed that scope in writing first.
  • Give us a reasonable time to respond before public disclosure (we aim to acknowledge within a few business days).

What this page is not

  • Not a bug bounty with fixed payouts (we may thank researchers; payment is not guaranteed).
  • Not permission to test third-party sites, customer sites, or systems you do not own.
  • Not the same as our website security check tool or paid penetration testing service.

Our security.txt

Machine-readable contact details live at /.well-known/security.txt. The Policy field points to this page.

What we run, so a report has context

This page is the disclosure channel for the systems behind trendibizz.com, the Concierge product, the Studio portal and the telephone assistant. It is not a marketing page and it is not a bug bounty with a cash table. It exists so that a researcher who finds something has a route to a person who can fix it.

The estate in brief: a public website and its tools, an API and database behind the Studio portal, a telephony service with a carrier trunk, a social publishing worker, and the operational tooling that deploys and monitors them. Each has its own exposure, so tell us which one you are reporting on.

How a report is handled

  1. You send it to the address in our security.txt, with enough detail to reproduce: the URL or number, the request, the response, and what you expected.
  2. We acknowledge as soon as a person has read it, usually within a few working days.
  3. We triage and tell you whether we consider it in scope, and what we intend to do.
  4. We fix, then verify the fix ourselves before calling it closed, and we tell you when it ships.
  5. We credit you only with your permission, and never publish a report that identifies a customer or exposes live data.

What is in scope, and what is not

In scope: authentication and authorisation flaws, injection, cross-tenant data exposure, secrets in public assets, and anything that lets an unauthenticated party act as a customer or an owner. Out of scope: rate-limit findings without demonstrated impact, automated scanner output with no working proof, social engineering of staff or customers, physical access, sustained denial of service, and reports about third-party services we only integrate with (those go to the vendor).

Please do not test against a real customer’s workspace, and do not access, modify, download or retain data that is not yours. If a test would cross that line, stop and email us first. We will not pursue action against a researcher who reports in good faith, stays inside this scope and gives us a reasonable window to fix before publishing.

What we publish

We publish findings about our own estate once they are fixed and once publishing cannot expose a customer: what was wrong, what changed and what the evidence was. The penetration testing service is separate from this page — a review we do for you, not a channel for reporting on us — and the security check tool is a free starting point for your own site.

Questions

Do you pay for vulnerability reports?

There is no cash bounty. We credit researchers who ask to be credited, and we tell you plainly if a finding is out of scope rather than negotiating.

How long before you fix something?

It depends on severity. A cross-tenant data exposure or authentication bypass is treated as urgent; a hardening suggestion may wait for the next planned change. We tell you which after triage.

Can I test the Studio portal or the telephone service?

Only against your own workspace or a tenant you control, and not with data that is not yours. If a test needs anything beyond that, email first and we will set a boundary in writing.
