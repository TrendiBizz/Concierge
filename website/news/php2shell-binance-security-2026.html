Two incidents, two doors in: what PHP2Shell and the Binance theft mean for business websites

By Trendi, Published 2 November 2026, Updated 4 November 2026

Facts as of 4 November 2026; Binance figures may change

Unpatched server software and compromised staff devices featured in this fortnight's major security stories, and Binance has now described a months-long attack on its staff. Here is a calm checklist for site owners.

Two unrelated security events have drawn global attention. Trendi, which helps businesses with SEO, AI visibility and website security, sets out what is known and what site owners can do.

What happened

PHP2Shell: flaws in PHP itself

CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 can allow unauthenticated remote code execution, and CERT-EU rates both CVSS 9.2 [5]. They are in PHP itself (the language runtime), not in a plugin [1]. PHP says all PHP installations serving web requests are at risk, including PHP-FPM, Apache with mod_php, php-cgi, FrankenPHP and LiteSpeed, and it considers mod_php the highest risk [1].

PHP released fixes on 21 October 2026 in PHP 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 [1]. PHP 8.1 and older no longer receive updates from the PHP project, although some distributions and long-term-support vendors provide backported fixes [1].

Exploitation attempts began before the patches were released. Several security firms, including Cloudflare and Amazon, detected widespread attempts but described them as unsuccessful at the time [4]. On 24 October a researcher's honeypots recorded 35 shells launched across three honeypots, which SecurityWeek reported as the first confirmed code execution [3]. The researcher cautioned that the honeypots used a deliberately favourable single-worker mod_php setup, so the result is not a measure of how reliably the exploit works elsewhere [3].

On 26 October CISA added both flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue and urged organisations to patch immediately. It said a web application firewall (WAF) may provide some protection until systems are patched [2]. CISA's alert lists alerts from cyber security agencies in New Zealand, the UK, Australia and Canada [2].

PHP has also disclosed a further flaw, CVE-2026-115560, which it says needs an unusual combination of configuration and application code. Until the next regular release, it advises setting session.upload_progress.enabled=0 [1][5].

Binance: a record theft through staff devices

On 29 October, Binance lost about US$6.34 billion in digital assets from its hot wallets, according to its 3 November findings [11]. CNBC describes it as the largest crypto theft on record [7].

Binance's initial investigation suggested the incident may involve a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorised withdrawal transactions. Co-CEO Richard Teng later said the attack involved a more complex process than the misuse of a single engineer's permissions, according to CNBC [7]. Binance's 3 November findings describe a multi-stage campaign. In June an engineer installed a tampered developer tool; in September a fake recruiter got malware onto a second engineer's work computer through a coding 'assignment'; on 20 October the attacker used an internal AI tool to reach other engineers' machines and a container deployment token; and on 29 October that token gave access to the internal wallet-signing service. Binance says the hot wallet used multi-party computation (MPC), so there was no single private key [11].

Binance initially said about US$3 billion had been frozen on-chain [6]; its 3 November findings put the amount frozen or intercepted at about US$2.85 billion, subject to reconciliation [11]. It temporarily suspended withdrawals and said it, not its users, would absorb the losses [6][7]. Binance says it had replenished 98% of the impacted token assets as of 31 October; this is Binance's own money covering users, not recovered funds [11]. Binance began resuming withdrawals in phases on 1 November, starting with bitcoin [7].

On 31 October the FBI said North Korea was responsible for the theft of more than US$6 billion from Binance, activity it calls "TraderTraitor" [8].

Why it matters for ordinary business websites

These events point to two ways in: unpatched server software, and compromised staff devices and credentials. Binance's findings point to the second: staff devices compromised through a tampered installer and a fake job interview [11]. Small business sites face the first one directly.

CISA notes that PHP is often a component of other software, so it may not be obvious where it runs [2]. In the same week, CISA added actively exploited flaws in Adobe Commerce and Magento (CVE-2026-48356, known as PolyShell, a file-upload flaw) and GitLab (CVE-2026-97229) to its catalogue [9][10]. The Hacker News reports that Sansec saw attackers use PolyShell to breach hundreds of online stores [9].

Six steps every site should take now

  1. Patch PHP and dependencies. Move to a fixed PHP release, update your CMS and plugins, and move off PHP 8.1 or older, or get a backported fix from your host or vendor [1]. If you do not manage the server yourself, ask your host which PHP version you run.
  2. Use a WAF as a stopgap. It may give some protection until patching is done, but it is not a substitute for patching [2].
  3. Set security headers. Deploy a Content Security Policy (CSP) and HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) alongside other standard headers. You can see which headers your homepage sends with our website security check.
  4. Keep tested, offline backups. Restore from them regularly so you know they work.
  5. Turn on MFA and protect devices. Cover admin, hosting, DNS, email and developer accounts, and staff laptops. Treat coding 'assignments' from recruiters and tools found through ads as untrusted.
  6. Monitor and log. Watch for unexpected processes and files on your servers, such as PHP worker processes launching shells or downloading tools [2].
