What a virtual receptionist does

A virtual receptionist answers the routine calls and messages a business receives, so the team can keep working. Concierge answers from your own business information, captures the caller’s name and contact details, offers agreed appointment times and records the conversation.

It is not a call centre and it is not a person. It handles routine questions and passes complex or sensitive conversations to your team with the context.

Built for businesses that answer worldwide

Concierge works in your timezone, with your opening hours and your local number. It can cover after-hours enquiries, weekends and busy periods. For phone calls, a local number and voice provider are required, and each channel is tested before activation.

It also works for businesses outside my country; the available channels depend on the providers in your country.

Virtual receptionist vs a human answering service

A human answering service charges per call, per minute or per agent hour. Concierge is a fixed monthly subscription, answers every hour you enable, and records the enquiry in your CRM. People remain better at negotiation, complaints and high-value conversations; the practical answer is often both.

What a virtual receptionist is, without the sales language

A virtual receptionist answers the phone and the inbox on behalf of a business, from somewhere other than the business's own desk. Traditionally that meant an agency with people on shift; now it can mean software that answers from your own information and hands over when a person is needed. The distinction matters because the two are priced, staffed and supervised very differently.

Concierge is the second kind. It answers website chat, phone, email and SMS enquiries inside the hours you enable, captures the contact details with permission, offers the appointment times you allow, and records everything in a CRM your team can see. A human answering service is still better at negotiation, complaints and anything emotionally complex — we say so on AI vs human answering service.

The three questions that decide whether it fits

  1. How repetitive are your enquiries? If most calls are hours, location, availability and how to book, an assistant covers the majority. If most are bespoke technical questions, it will hand over constantly and you are paying for a switchboard you still staff.
  2. How many arrive outside your open hours? After-hours cover is where a virtual receptionist earns its keep, because the alternative is usually voicemail that nobody hears until morning.
  3. Can you write down the rules? Which enquiries are urgent, which numbers to reach, which appointments are bookable. If the answer is "it depends", the rules become the project — and we would rather do that work with you than let the assistant guess.

What you keep, and what you must still staff

You keep your number, your brand, your hours and your data. You still staff the conversations that need judgement: complaints, pricing negotiation, clinical or legal questions, and anyone who explicitly asks for a person. The assistant is instructed to hand those over with the transcript attached rather than improvise.

What changes is the tail: the 9pm enquiry, the Saturday morning booking, the question answered while your one receptionist is with a customer. Those become recorded, owned and followable within the same workspace.
