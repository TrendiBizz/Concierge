THE SMALL BUSINESS FIELD GUIDE

AI search glossary

This page is a glossary of terms used in AI search, from generative engine optimisation to IndexNow. Each entry gives a plain definition and, where relevant, links to a fuller guide.

By Trendi · Published 2 November 2026

AI search terms are often used loosely. These short definitions explain what each term means and what it does not mean.

Generative engine optimisation (GEO)

Generative engine optimisation (GEO, also spelt optimization) is the practice of making content clear and accessible so AI answer tools can understand and reference it. Read the full guide to generative engine optimisation.

Answer engine optimisation (AEO)

Answer engine optimisation (AEO, also spelt optimization) is the practice of structuring content so it can be used as a direct answer by AI assistants and search features. See answer engine optimisation in the main guide.

Answer engine

An answer engine is a search tool that replies with a direct written answer instead of only a list of links. AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Perplexity work this way, as do AI search features inside traditional search engines. The answer is usually composed from several sources, and some tools show those sources as links. The practice of preparing content for these tools is called answer engine optimisation. To see how a business is named in these answers, read about getting mentioned in ChatGPT and Perplexity.

llms.txt

llms.txt is a proposed plain-text file that lists a site's key pages for AI tools, aimed at crawl readiness rather than ranking. It is not a ranking signal, AI tool support is limited and unconfirmed, and Google Search does not use it, as explained in the llms.txt guide.

AI crawler

An AI crawler is an automated bot that fetches web pages for an AI company, either to collect content for model training or to power AI search results. For example, OpenAI's GPTBot and OAI-SearchBot can be treated separately in robots.txt, which you can check with the AI crawler robots.txt checker.

AI Overviews

AI Overviews are Google's AI-generated summaries shown on some search results pages, with links to supporting web pages. Google shows an overview only when its systems judge it adds to the classic results, so it often does not appear. To be eligible as a supporting link, a page must be indexed and eligible to show with a snippet, with no extra technical requirements or special schema. Appearing is never guaranteed. Clicks and impressions from AI features are counted in the Search Console Performance report under the Web search type (Google's guide to AI features). The wider topic is AI visibility.

AI Mode

AI Mode is Google's AI search experience for questions that need more exploration, reasoning or comparison. It supports follow-up questions and gives AI-written responses with links to supporting websites. It may use query fan-out, which means running related searches across subtopics. It can use different models and show different links from AI Overviews. Google decides which pages it links, so no one can promise a place in AI Mode (Google's guide to AI features). It is one part of AI visibility.

LLM citation

An LLM citation is a source link shown with an AI answer, pointing to the page the information came from. It differs from a brand mention, where an AI names a business without linking to it. The two are separate outcomes: an answer can name a business without a link, or link to a page without naming the business in the text, so they are reported separately. Read about being mentioned in ChatGPT and Perplexity, and the broader idea of AI visibility.

AI visibility

AI visibility is whether and how a business appears in answers from AI assistants and AI search, from being indexed, to being found, to being mentioned or linked. Mentions and links are separate outcomes and are reported separately, as the full AI visibility guide explains.

Zero-click search

Zero-click search is a search where the person gets what they need on the results page and does not click through to any website. Common examples include featured snippets, answer boxes, map packs and AI Overviews. For a business, this means a search can show its name, hours or an answer without producing a visit. It does not mean the business is absent from results. If a business is not appearing at all, see why a business may not be showing on Google.

Crawl budget

Crawl budget is the set of URLs Google can and wants to crawl on a site, set by crawl capacity limit and crawl demand. Crawl capacity limit is how much Google can fetch without overloading a server, and crawl demand is how much Google wants to crawl. Google's crawl budget guide is aimed at very large or fast-changing sites. For most sites, keeping the sitemap up to date and checking the Page Indexing report is enough. Timing questions are covered in how long indexing takes.

IndexNow

IndexNow is an open protocol for telling participating search engines that URLs were added, updated or deleted. Participating engines include Bing, Yandex, Naver, Seznam.cz, Yep and others listed on indexnow.org, and one submission is shared with all of them. Submitting does not guarantee crawling or indexing. Google is not a participating engine and does not use IndexNow. IndexNow is a crawl notice, not a ranking factor. See the IndexNow FAQ and how long indexing takes.
