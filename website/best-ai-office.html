How to choose the best AI receptionist (AI office)

There is no single “best” AI receptionist or AI office. The right one depends on the channels you need, how it books, where your data lives and what happens when it does not know the answer. Here is how to compare them.

What to test before you buy

Ask each provider to answer real questions from your website, capture a lead, book a real appointment and show you the CRM record. Check what happens when the answer is not in the knowledge base, and how a person takes over.

Confirm which channels are actually live and tested: website chat, email, SMS and phone are separate integrations.

Pricing models

Some services charge per call or per minute; others charge a monthly subscription. Compare the total cost, including phone and SMS provider charges, and check the minimum term. Concierge is USD $250 a month with a 3-month minimum; provider charges are separate.

Data, consent and control

Ask where conversations and contact details are stored, who can access them, how long they are kept and whether recording asks the caller first. You should own or control your CRM data and be able to export it.

Limits to insist on

No provider should promise leads, sales or rankings. The assistant should say when it does not know, avoid inventing prices, and hand complex conversations to a person. Check that a human signs off quotations and outbound messages.

AI receptionist and AI office questions

What is the best AI receptionist or AI office?

There is no universal best. Compare channels, booking, data control, consent and total cost for your business. Concierge is one option, set up around your website and CRM.

How much should an AI office cost?

Expect a monthly subscription plus provider charges for phone and SMS. Concierge is USD $250 a month with a 3-month minimum.

Can it answer phone calls?

Only when a number and voice provider are connected and tested. Website chat works without one.

Does it replace staff?

No. It handles routine answering, capture and booking and passes complex conversations to people.

Does it guarantee more leads?

No. It helps make sure enquiries are answered and recorded; demand and conversion are outside its control.

How do I compare AI offices fairly?

Give each one the same live test with your own content: three real questions, one your site does not answer, one that needs a person, and one that asks for a discount. Score accuracy, honesty about gaps, handover, the record produced, and setup time.

What should an AI office never do?

It should never invent a price, policy, availability or medical, legal or financial advice, and it should never keep a customer in a loop when a person is needed. Ask each vendor what their tool refuses to do.

Do I need a phone number to start?

No. Website chat and email capture work without one, and they are the fastest way to test whether the assistant’s answers are good enough. Phone and SMS need a provider that operates in your country.

Is a three-month minimum normal?

For a service that is configured around your business, yes — setup, testing and content work happen before there is anything to bill for. What matters is that the term is stated up front and that you can export your data and leave at the end of it.

Start with the job, not the vendor

"Best" is not a property of a product; it is a match between a tool and the work you want it to take. Write down the three enquiries you most want answered without you: a new customer asking whether you cover their area, someone wanting to book next week, and a question about price or lead time. If a candidate cannot handle those three in a live test, the rest of the comparison does not matter.

The order of testing matters too. Channels first (what is actually live, not what is on the roadmap), then booking, then the data question, then price. Most buyers do it backwards: they compare monthly fees first, then discover the channel they needed is unavailable in their country.

The seven questions that separate the products

  1. Which channels are live today, in my country? Website chat, email, SMS and phone are four separate integrations. Ask for a demonstration on a real number, not a video.
  2. What happens when it does not know? A good assistant says so and offers a handover. A bad one invents a policy, a price or a delivery time, and you find out from a customer.
  3. How does a person take over? Mid-conversation, or only after the customer asks? Test both.
  4. Where does my data live, who can see it, and how do I get it out? Export should not be a support ticket.
  5. What is the total cost? Subscription plus per-minute or per-message provider charges plus setup. Ask for a worked example at your call volume.
  6. What is the minimum term and how do I leave? A three-month minimum is normal for a configured service; a lock-in with no exit is not.
  7. What does the assistant refuse to do? The refusals tell you how it was built. Ours will not quote, negotiate, give clinical or legal advice, or promise availability it cannot verify.

How to run a fair trial

Give every candidate the same test. Use your own website content and your own customer questions, and include one question your site does not answer, one that needs a person, and one that tries to get a discount. Score each on: answer accuracy, whether it admitted the gap, the handover, the record it produced, and how long the whole thing took to set up. Two of the four scores usually decide it, and neither is the demo.

The trap to avoid is judging the voice. A pleasant voice with invented answers is worse than a plain one that says "I do not know, may I take your number". If you want the wider comparison, read AI answering service vs human answering service.
